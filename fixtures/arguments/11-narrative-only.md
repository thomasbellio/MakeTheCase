# The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Winter

The storm came in off the water on the second night of December, and by morning the causeway to the mainland was gone.

Marta Ilves had kept the light on Corran Point for thirty-one years. She knew the sound the tower made when the wind turned northeast, a low hum in the iron stair rail, and she knew it meant three days of weather at least. She banked the stove, filled the lamp reservoir, and carried the last of the dry wood up from the shed before the rain turned to sleet.

On the third day a fishing boat appeared at the edge of the fog, running without lights. Marta watched it through the glass as it turned, too late, toward the rocks below the point. She rang the fog bell until her shoulders burned. The boat came about, slowly, and slipped past the reef with what looked like a yard to spare.

It was spring before she learned the boat's name. A letter arrived with the first supply launch, written in a careful hand by the skipper's daughter. Her father had heard the bell, she wrote. He had not known anyone still lived on the point.

Marta read the letter twice, folded it into the logbook beside the entry for the third of December, and went up to trim the wick.
