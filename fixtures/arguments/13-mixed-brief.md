# Appellant's Brief: Rivera v. Westbrook Department of Housing

## Introduction

Appellant Daniela Rivera submitted a public records request to the Westbrook Department of Housing. The Department never produced records, never denied the request, and never extended its deadline. Under the Westbrook Public Records Act, its silence was a denial, and Ms. Rivera was entitled to appeal. The Records Appeals Board should deny the Department's motion to dismiss her appeal as premature.

## Statement of Facts

On March 3, Ms. Rivera emailed a records request to the Department's designated records officer, asking for all inspection reports for the Calloway Apartments from the previous three years. (Request, R. at 4.)

The next morning, March 4, the Department's records system sent an automated email to Ms. Rivera. The email read, in full: "Your request has been received and assigned tracking number PR-2291." (Auto-Reply, R. at 5.)

Ms. Rivera heard nothing further. On March 20, she emailed the records officer asking about the status of her request. She received no reply. (Follow-Up Email, R. at 6.) On March 27, she called the Department's main line and left a voicemail. No one returned the call. (Rivera Aff. ¶ 7, R. at 9.)

As of the date of this brief, the Department has not produced any records, has not denied the request, and has not sent any notice extending its time to respond. (Rivera Aff. ¶ 8, R. at 9.)

## Procedural History

Ms. Rivera filed her appeal with the Records Appeals Board on April 2. (Notice of Appeal, R. at 1.) On April 16, the Department moved to dismiss the appeal as premature, arguing that its March 4 email was a response to the request and that the request therefore remained pending. (Mot. to Dismiss, R. at 12.) This brief opposes that motion.

## Argument

### I. The Department's silence was a denial under Section 5(c).

Section 5(c) of the Public Records Act provides that if an agency fails to respond to a request within ten business days of receiving it, the request is deemed denied. Westbrook Pub. Rec. Act § 5(c).

The Department received Ms. Rivera's request on March 3. Ten business days after March 3 was March 17. As set out in the Statement of Facts, the Department produced no records, issued no denial, and sent no extension notice by March 17, or at any time since.

The Department contends that its automated March 4 email was a response. It was not. Section 2(f) of the Act defines a "response" as "the production of responsive records, a written denial stating the grounds for denial, or a written notice extending the time to respond." Westbrook Pub. Rec. Act § 2(f). The March 4 email did none of those things. It acknowledged receipt and assigned a tracking number, nothing more.

Because the Department did not respond within ten business days, the request was deemed denied on March 17.

### II. Ms. Rivera's appeal was therefore timely and not premature.

Section 7(a) of the Act permits a requester to appeal to the Records Appeals Board after a request is denied, including a request deemed denied under Section 5(c). Westbrook Pub. Rec. Act § 7(a). Ms. Rivera's request was deemed denied on March 17, and she appealed on April 2, after that denial. Her appeal was not premature.

## Conclusion

The Board should deny the Department's motion to dismiss and proceed to the merits of Ms. Rivera's appeal.
