# The Non-Compete Agreement Is Unenforceable Against Ms. Reyes

Meridian Analytics seeks to enforce a non-compete agreement against its former employee, Lucia Reyes. The agreement is unenforceable because it was never supported by independent consideration.

Meridian hired Ms. Reyes as a data engineer in January 2021. (Reyes Decl. ¶ 2.) In March 2023, more than two years into her employment, Meridian presented her with the non-compete agreement and she signed it. (Reyes Decl. ¶ 4; Agreement, Ex. A.) Meridian admits that Ms. Reyes received no raise, bonus, promotion, or payment in connection with signing. (Meridian Resp. to Interrog. No. 7.)

Because Ms. Reyes was already employed when she signed, and because she received nothing else in exchange, the only consideration Meridian provided for the agreement was her continued employment.

Under _Dalton v. Kesler Manufacturing_, when an employee signs a non-compete after employment has already begun, continued employment alone is not independent consideration. _Dalton v. Kesler Mfg._, 412 W.3d 88, 93 (Westbrook 2019). It follows that the agreement Ms. Reyes signed lacked independent consideration.

Section 22 of the Westbrook Labor Code provides that a non-compete agreement is enforceable only if it is supported by independent consideration. Westbrook Lab. Code § 22. Because the agreement lacked independent consideration, it is unenforceable against Ms. Reyes.
