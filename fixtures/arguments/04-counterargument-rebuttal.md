# Ms. Lin's Water Damage Claim Is Covered

Ms. Grace Lin's homeowner's policy with Northfield Mutual covers the water damage to her basement.

Section 2 of the policy covers "sudden and accidental discharge of water from within a plumbing system" in the insured dwelling. (Policy, Ex. A, § 2.) On the night of February 9, a supply line beneath Ms. Lin's kitchen sink burst, and water ran down through the floor into the basement over the course of several hours. (Lin Decl. ¶¶ 3–5; Plumber's Report, Ex. B.) The discharge was sudden and accidental, and it came from within the home's plumbing system. The damage therefore falls within Section 2.

Northfield denied the claim. Its denial letter asserts that the flood exclusion in Section 5(d) bars coverage, reasoning that because water accumulated in the basement, the loss was caused by flooding. (Denial Letter, Ex. C.)

Northfield is wrong for two reasons.

First, the water was not flood water. Section 5(d) defines "flood" as "surface water, waves, or overflow of a body of water originating outside the dwelling." (Policy § 5(d).) The plumber's report establishes that all of the water came from the burst supply line inside the house. (Ex. B at 2.) None of it originated outside the dwelling.

Second, Northfield's reasoning does not follow even on its own terms. That water collected in the basement says nothing about whether it was a flood. The policy defines flood by where the water came from, not by where it ended up. Water from a burst pipe that pools in a basement is still water from a burst pipe.

Because the flood exclusion does not apply, nothing removes the loss from Section 2's coverage, and Northfield must cover Ms. Lin's claim.
