# IN THE SUPERIOR COURT OF WESTBROOK COUNTY

**BRIGHTWATER YOGA STUDIO, LLC,**
Plaintiff,

v.

**PEMBERTON HOLDINGS, INC.,**
Defendant.

Case No. CV-0418-22

## PLAINTIFF'S MEMORANDUM IN SUPPORT OF MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

## Introduction

For more than four years, Brightwater Yoga Studio has operated out of Suite 110 at the Pemberton Commons shopping center. Its lease gave it one valuable right: the option to renew for a second five-year term. Brightwater exercised that option on May 2 by delivering written notice to Pemberton Holdings, inside the window the lease allows and at a time when Brightwater was not in default. Pemberton now refuses to honor the renewal and has begun marketing Suite 110 to other tenants.

Pemberton offers two reasons for refusing to recognize the renewal. First, it says the notice arrived late. Second, it says Brightwater was in default because of an exterior sign. Neither reason survives the undisputed record. Brightwater delivered its notice on May 2, two days before the window closed. Pemberton's own property manager approved the sign in writing. And even if the sign had been unapproved, the lease does not treat a non-monetary breach as a "Default" until the landlord gives written notice and the tenant fails to cure within 30 days, and Pemberton never gave any such notice.

Because there is no genuine dispute of material fact, Brightwater is entitled to summary judgment declaring that it validly exercised its renewal option, and to an injunction barring Pemberton from leasing Suite 110 to anyone else during the renewal term.

## Statement of Undisputed Facts

### The Lease and the Renewal Option

1. Brightwater and Pemberton entered into a commercial lease for Suite 110 of Pemberton Commons (the "Lease"). The Initial Term of the Lease runs for five years and expires on October 31 of this year. (Lease, Ex. 1, §§ 1.1, 3.1.)

2. Section 4.2 of the Lease grants Brightwater an option to renew for one additional five-year term. To exercise the option, Brightwater must deliver written notice to Pemberton "not earlier than two hundred seventy (270) days and not later than one hundred eighty (180) days prior to the expiration of the Initial Term." (Lease § 4.2.)

3. Section 4.2 further provides that the option may be exercised only if Brightwater "is not in Default at the time it delivers notice of exercise." (Lease § 4.2.)

4. The 270-day point before October 31 is February 3. The 180-day point before October 31 is May 4. The renewal window therefore ran from February 3 through May 4. (Lease §§ 3.1, 4.2.)

5. Section 22 of the Lease provides that notices to Pemberton are to be delivered by hand, by overnight courier, or by certified mail to Pemberton's office at 75 Granite Avenue, and that a notice is "deemed delivered upon receipt as evidenced by a signed delivery receipt." (Lease § 22.)

### Brightwater's Notice of Renewal

6. On May 1, Brightwater's owner, Priya Raman, signed a written notice stating that Brightwater "hereby exercises its option to renew the Lease for the Renewal Term pursuant to Section 4.2." (Notice of Renewal, Ex. 2.)

7. Brightwater sent the notice by overnight courier to Pemberton's office at 75 Granite Avenue. The courier's delivery receipt shows that the package was delivered on May 2 at 10:14 a.m. and was signed for by "J. Whitcomb," Pemberton's receptionist. (Courier Delivery Receipt, Ex. 3; Whitcomb Dep. 14:2–15:9, Ex. 4.)

8. Pemberton's internal mail log records the notice as received on May 6. (Pemberton Mail Log, Ex. 5.) Ms. Whitcomb testified that packages are often logged several days after they arrive, when the office manager processes the week's mail. (Whitcomb Dep. 17:3–18:11.)

### Pemberton's Conduct After Receiving the Notice

9. On May 20, Pemberton's property manager, Dana Kuehn, emailed Ms. Raman. The email stated: "Thanks for your renewal. Attached is the CAM budget for the renewal term, and we'll need an updated insurance certificate covering the new term by September 1." (Kuehn Email of May 20, Ex. 6.)

10. On June 3, Ms. Kuehn sent Brightwater a renewal-term rent schedule showing monthly base rent for each year of the Renewal Term. (Rent Schedule, Ex. 7.)

11. On July 14, Pemberton's counsel sent Brightwater a letter asserting for the first time that the renewal notice was untimely and that Brightwater had been in default because of its exterior sign. (Letter of July 14, Ex. 8.)

### The Exterior Sign

12. Section 11.3 of the Lease requires Pemberton's prior written consent before Brightwater installs any exterior signage. (Lease § 11.3.)

13. On March 9, Ms. Raman emailed Ms. Kuehn a rendering of a new exterior sign and asked for approval. Ms. Kuehn replied the same day: "Looks great, approved. Go ahead and schedule the install." (Raman–Kuehn Email Chain of March 9, Ex. 9.)

14. Brightwater installed the sign on March 30. (Raman Decl. ¶ 12, Ex. 10.)

### The Lease's Definition of Default

15. Section 19.1 of the Lease defines "Default." A Default occurs if (a) Brightwater fails to pay any installment of rent within five days after it is due, or (b) Brightwater fails to perform any other obligation under the Lease and that failure continues for 30 days after Pemberton gives Brightwater written notice of the failure. (Lease § 19.1.)

16. Before May 2, Pemberton never sent Brightwater any written notice of any failure to perform an obligation under the Lease. (Pemberton Resp. to Req. for Admis. No. 3, Ex. 11.)

17. Brightwater has paid every installment of rent on time throughout the Lease term.

### Pemberton's Marketing of Suite 110

18. In August, Pemberton listed Suite 110 for lease on a commercial real estate website, describing it as "available November 1." (Listing, Ex. 12.)

19. Brightwater has approximately 640 active members, most of whom live within three miles of Pemberton Commons. (Raman Decl. ¶ 20.)

## Procedural History

Brightwater filed this action on August 22, seeking a declaratory judgment that it validly exercised its renewal option and an injunction barring Pemberton from leasing Suite 110 to another tenant during the Renewal Term. (Compl., Dkt. 1.) Pemberton answered on September 15, asserting that the renewal notice was untimely and that Brightwater was in default when it gave notice. (Answer, Dkt. 9.) The parties have completed discovery on these issues. (Scheduling Order, Dkt. 14.)

## Standard of Review

Summary judgment is appropriate where there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. Westbrook R. Civ. P. 56(a). A dispute is genuine only if the evidence would allow a reasonable factfinder to return a verdict for the non-moving party. *Ashby v. Corlett*, 288 W.3d 410, 414 (Westbrook 2014). The interpretation of an unambiguous contract is a question of law for the court. *Fenwick Partners v. Lowry*, 301 W.3d 77, 81 (Westbrook 2016).

## Argument

### I. Brightwater validly exercised its renewal option.

Section 4.2 sets out what Brightwater had to do to renew: deliver written notice within the renewal window, at a time when it was not in Default. Brightwater satisfied both requirements. Its notice was timely (Part I.A), and it was not in Default when it gave notice (Part I.B). Brightwater therefore validly exercised its option to renew.

#### A. Brightwater's notice was timely.

##### 1. Brightwater delivered its notice on May 2, within the renewal window.

The renewal window closed on May 4. (Undisputed Fact ¶ 4.) Brightwater delivered its notice on May 2.

The Lease answers the question of when a notice is delivered. Under Section 22, a notice sent by overnight courier to 75 Granite Avenue is "deemed delivered upon receipt as evidenced by a signed delivery receipt." (Lease § 22.) The signed courier receipt shows receipt at Pemberton's office on May 2 at 10:14 a.m., and Pemberton's own receptionist confirmed that the signature is hers. (Undisputed Fact ¶ 7.) Under the plain terms of Section 22, the notice was delivered on May 2.

Pemberton's reliance on its internal mail log is misplaced. The log records the date the notice was processed, May 6, not the date it was received. Ms. Whitcomb testified that the office routinely logs packages several days after they arrive. (Undisputed Fact ¶ 8.) More fundamentally, the Lease does not make Pemberton's internal log the measure of delivery; it makes the signed delivery receipt the measure. An internal administrative entry cannot override the method of proof the parties chose in Section 22.

Because Pemberton received Brightwater's notice on May 2, two days before the window closed, the notice was timely.

##### 2. In the alternative, Pemberton waived any objection to timeliness.

Even if there were some doubt about when the notice was delivered, Pemberton waived any objection to its timeliness by treating the renewal as effective.

After receiving the notice, Pemberton did not object. Instead, its property manager thanked Brightwater "for your renewal," sent the CAM budget "for the renewal term," and requested an insurance certificate "covering the new term." (Undisputed Fact ¶ 9.) Two weeks later, Pemberton sent a rent schedule for each year of the Renewal Term. (Undisputed Fact ¶ 10.) Pemberton raised timeliness for the first time on July 14, more than two months after it received the notice and after it had repeatedly treated the renewal as done. (Undisputed Fact ¶ 11.)

Pemberton's conduct after receiving the notice treated the renewal as effective. Pemberton therefore waived any objection to the timeliness of Brightwater's notice.

Either way, the timeliness requirement of Section 4.2 is satisfied: Brightwater's notice was delivered on May 2, inside the window, and even if it had not been, Pemberton's conduct waived the objection.

#### B. Brightwater was not in Default when it delivered its notice.

The second requirement of Section 4.2 is that Brightwater not be "in Default at the time it delivers notice of exercise." Brightwater gave notice on May 2, and on May 2 it was not in Default.

Section 19.1 recognizes only two kinds of Default: a failure to pay rent within five days after it is due, and a failure to perform some other obligation that continues for 30 days after written notice from Pemberton. (Undisputed Fact ¶ 15.) Neither existed on May 2.

There was no monetary Default. Brightwater has paid every installment of rent on time throughout the Lease term. (Undisputed Fact ¶ 17.)

There was no non-monetary Default either. Pemberton contends that Brightwater's exterior sign put it in Default, because Section 11.3 requires Pemberton's prior written consent for exterior signage. That contention fails for two independent reasons.

First, Pemberton consented in writing. On March 9, three weeks before installation, Pemberton's property manager reviewed a rendering of the sign and replied, "Looks great, approved. Go ahead and schedule the install." (Undisputed Fact ¶ 13.) That is prior written consent within the meaning of Section 11.3. Brightwater did not breach Section 11.3 at all.

Second, even if the sign had been installed without consent, an unconsented sign would not have put Brightwater in Default. Pemberton's argument assumes that any breach of the Lease is a Default. The Lease says otherwise. Under Section 19.1(b), a failure to perform a non-monetary obligation becomes a Default only if it continues for 30 days after Pemberton gives written notice of it. Pemberton admits that it never gave Brightwater written notice of any failure to perform before May 2. (Undisputed Fact ¶ 16.) Without notice, the 30-day cure period never began, and no Default could have arisen. Pemberton cannot convert an alleged breach into a Default by skipping the step the Lease requires.

Because Brightwater had no monetary Default and no non-monetary Default on May 2, it was not in Default when it delivered its notice.

#### C. Brightwater satisfied every requirement of Section 4.2.

Brightwater's notice was timely, and Brightwater was not in Default when it delivered the notice on May 2. Those are the only conditions Section 4.2 places on the renewal option. Brightwater validly exercised its option to renew the Lease for the Renewal Term, and it is entitled to a declaratory judgment saying so.

### II. Brightwater is entitled to a permanent injunction.

A permanent injunction is warranted where the plaintiff has succeeded on the merits, would suffer irreparable harm without an injunction, and the balance of hardships and the public interest favor relief. *Marlow v. Tenney Bros.*, 296 W.3d 233, 240 (Westbrook 2015).

Brightwater has succeeded on the merits for the reasons set out in Part I.

Brightwater would likely suffer irreparable harm without an injunction. Pemberton has listed Suite 110 as available on November 1. (Undisputed Fact ¶ 18.) If Pemberton signs a lease with another tenant, Brightwater may be unable to recover possession of the space even after a final judgment, because the new tenant's rights may intervene. Brightwater's business depends heavily on its location: most of its roughly 640 members live within three miles of Pemberton Commons. (Undisputed Fact ¶ 19.) A forced relocation would probably cost Brightwater a substantial share of that membership, a loss that would be difficult to measure and likely impossible to fully compensate with damages.

The balance of hardships favors Brightwater. An injunction would require Pemberton only to honor the Lease it signed and to accept rent at the renewal rates it set itself. (Undisputed Fact ¶ 10.) Without an injunction, Brightwater may lose its business location altogether.

The public interest favors enforcing commercial leases according to their terms.

## Conclusion

Brightwater delivered its renewal notice on May 2, within the renewal window, and it was not in Default when it did so. Pemberton's objections fail on the undisputed record. The Court should grant summary judgment for Brightwater, declare that Brightwater validly exercised its option to renew the Lease for the Renewal Term, and permanently enjoin Pemberton from leasing Suite 110 to any other tenant during the Renewal Term.
