# The Commission Should Require a Supplemental Traffic Study

The Planning Commission should require Larkspur Development to submit a supplemental traffic study before it acts on the Larkspur Commons application, because the existing study likely understates peak-hour traffic at the Elm Street and Route 9 intersection.

The existing study appears to have been conducted during the week of April 14, when Westbrook public schools were on spring break. (Traffic Study, Ex. 5, at 3; School District Calendar, Ex. 6.) The Westbrook Department of Transportation's guidance notes that traffic counts taken during school holidays may understate typical weekday peak-hour volumes by as much as 20 percent. (DOT Traffic Study Guidance § 4.2, Ex. 7.)

If the counts were taken during the break, the study's peak-hour figures are probably too low. That matters here, because the study itself reports that the intersection already operates at 91 percent of capacity during the evening peak. (Ex. 5 at 11.) An undercount of even 10 percent could mean the intersection is at or near capacity today, and the additional traffic from Larkspur Commons might push it beyond capacity.

Given that the study likely understates existing traffic at an intersection that may already be near its limit, the Commission should require a supplemental study based on counts taken while schools are in session.
